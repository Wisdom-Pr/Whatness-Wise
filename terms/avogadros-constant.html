Avogadro's constant

Chemistry

Avogadro's constant is the proportionality factor that relates the number of constituent particles in a sample to the amount of substance in moles, defined as exactly 6.02214076 × 10^23 reciprocal moles.

Category
Physical constant
Identity
A proportionality factor relating the molar mass of a substance to the mass of a single molecule.
Mechanism
The ratio of the number of elementary entities to the amount of substance in moles.
Resolved
The standard definition often fails to explicitly state the relationship between the macroscopic mass (molar mass) and the microscopic mass (atomic mass), and it is often confused with the mole itself.

Usually defined as: The number of constituent particles that are contained in one mole of a given substance, approximately 6.022 x 10^23.
