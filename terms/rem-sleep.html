REM sleep

Preventive Medicine

REM sleep is a physiological state of high-frequency brain activity and vivid dreaming that is distinguished from wakefulness by the active suppression of voluntary muscle movement.

Category
Physiological state
Identity
A distinct phase of the sleep cycle characterized by high-frequency brain activity and muscle atonia.
Mechanism
The activation of the brainstem to suppress motor neurons while simultaneously stimulating cortical activity.
Resolved
The standard definition relies on a secondary symptom (eye movement) rather than the primary physiological state (atonia and cortical arousal) and fails to distinguish it from wakefulness.

Usually defined as: A stage of sleep characterized by rapid eye movement and vivid dreaming.
