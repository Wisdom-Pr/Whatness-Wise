Measles

Public Health

Measles is a highly contagious viral infection caused by the morbillivirus that produces a characteristic systemic rash and respiratory inflammation through the inhalation of infected airborne droplets.

Category
Infectious disease
Identity
A systemic viral infection caused by the measles morbillivirus.
Mechanism
Transmission via respiratory droplets that triggers an immune-mediated inflammatory response in the skin and respiratory mucosa.
Resolved
The standard definition fails to distinguish measles from other exanthematous viral rashes like rubella or roseola by omitting the specific viral etiology and the distinct respiratory-to-dermal progression.

Usually defined as: A highly contagious viral disease characterized by a red rash and fever.
