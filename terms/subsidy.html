Subsidy

Economics

A subsidy is a financial transfer from a government to a producer or consumer that lowers the cost of an economic activity to encourage specific market outcomes.

Category
Economic intervention
Identity
A financial transfer from a government to a producer or consumer
Mechanism
Reducing the effective cost of production or consumption to influence market behavior
Resolved
The standard definition fails to distinguish subsidies from direct welfare payments or general tax cuts, and it omits the mechanism of market distortion.

Usually defined as: A sum of money granted by the government or a public body to assist an industry or business so that the price of a commodity or service may remain low or competitive.
