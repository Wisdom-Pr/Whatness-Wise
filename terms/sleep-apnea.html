Sleep apnea

Preventive Medicine

Sleep apnea is a chronic respiratory disorder characterized by repeated, involuntary pauses in breathing during sleep caused by either physical airway obstruction or a failure of the brain to signal the muscles to breathe.

Category
Medical condition
Identity
A chronic respiratory disorder involving repeated cessations of airflow during sleep.
Mechanism
The physical obstruction of the upper airway or a failure of the brain to signal the muscles to breathe, resulting in intermittent oxygen deprivation.
Resolved
The standard definition fails to distinguish between the two primary types (obstructive vs. central) and does not specify the underlying cause of the breathing cessation.

Usually defined as: A sleep disorder characterized by pauses in breathing or periods of shallow breathing during sleep.
