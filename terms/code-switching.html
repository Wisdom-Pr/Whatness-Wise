Code-switching

Linguistics

Code-switching is the strategic alternation between two or more distinct languages or dialects within a single conversation to navigate social contexts, express identity, or improve communicative clarity.

Category
Sociolinguistic behavior
Identity
The alternating use of distinct linguistic varieties within a single communicative event.
Mechanism
The selection of specific lexical or grammatical features based on social context, audience, or intent to signal cultural identity or facilitate communication.
Resolved
The standard definition fails to distinguish code-switching from simple borrowing or interference, and it often ignores the strategic, communicative intent behind the alternation.

Usually defined as: The practice of alternating between two or more languages or varieties of language in conversation.
