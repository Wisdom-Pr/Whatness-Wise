Learning styles

Education

Learning styles are a framework of hypothesized individual preferences for specific sensory-based instructional methods that, despite widespread use, lack empirical evidence for improving academic performance when instruction is matched to those preferences.

Category
Educational theory
Identity
A proposed classification of individual cognitive preferences for instructional methods.
Mechanism
Categorizing students based on their self-reported or assessed tendencies to favor specific sensory modalities during information intake.
Resolved
The standard definition fails to mention that empirical evidence consistently shows that matching instruction to these alleged styles does not improve learning outcomes, making the term a classification of preference rather than a functional mechanism of cognition.

Usually defined as: The theory that individuals have preferred ways of absorbing, processing, and retaining information, such as visual, auditory, or kinesthetic.
